# Received Pronunciation (RP) Pronunciation Curriculum for Vinh
## Specially Designed for a Vietnamese Speaker Improving Social Fluency

This curriculum is based on **Peter Roach’s *English Phonetics and Phonology (3rd Edition)*** and is customized to address the phonological transfer issues of native Vietnamese speakers, with a focus on word-ending sounds and everyday social conversation vocabulary.

---

## Part 1: The Linguistic Challenge (Vietnamese -> English RP)

To teach Vinh effectively, you need to understand *why* he drops ending sounds in social settings:
1. **Vietnamese Syllable Structure:** Vietnamese syllables only allow a very small set of final consonants: stops `/p, t, k/` and nasals `/m, n, ŋ/`. There are **no final fricatives** (`/s, z, f, v, ʃ, θ/`), **no final affricates** (`/tʃ, dʒ/`), and **no consonant clusters** (like `/st, ts, nd, kt/`).
2. **Unreleased Stops:** In Vietnamese, final stops are *unreleased* (the lips/tongue stay closed, and no air escapes). In English (especially RP), final stops must be audibly released.
3. **Syllable-Timed Rhythm:** Vietnamese gives equal time to every syllable. English is *stress-timed*, squeezing unstressed syllables into weak forms and schwas. If Vinh pronounces every syllable with equal weight, he will run out of time to pronounce complex final consonant clusters.
4. **RP Markers:** RP is *non-rhotic* (do not pronounce "r" after vowels: *dinner* is `/ˈdɪnə/`), uses the long open bath vowel `/ɑː/` (*bath* `/bɑːθ/, *garden* `/ˈɡɑːdn̩/`), and features clean, distinct contrast between voiceless (fortis) and voiced (lenis) final consonants.

## Part 2: The 9-Lesson Curriculum Arch

### Lesson 1: The Social Outing & Articulator Awareness
* **Focus:** Student motivation, baseline diagnostic, and mastering control of the mouth’s physical articulators (Roach Chapters 1 & 2).
* **Why it matters to Vinh:** Clear speech structures establish immediate friendliness and connection in social conversations. Vinh must treat ending consonants with the same precision as pronouncing a friend's name.
* **Articulatory Goal:** Learning the difference between the **alveolar ridge** (the bumpy part behind upper teeth), the **hard palate**, and the **soft palate (velum)**, and how to control them.

#### 1. Warm-up Exercises (3 mins)
* **The Velvet Yawn:** Have Vinh yawn to lift his soft palate. This creates a resonant oral cavity, essential for British RP vowels.
* **Alveolar Tap:** Have Vinh tap his tongue tip rapidly behind his upper teeth (saying "t-t-t-t-t") to locate the alveolar ridge.
* **Tongue Protrusion:** Stretch the tongue straight out, then pull it back to touch the back of the roof of the mouth.

#### 2. Tutor Script & Lesson Plan (20 mins)
* *Introduction:* "Vinh, in social conversations, small details in speech make you sound much more friendly and engaging. Dropping the end of words is like cutting off the end of your stories. Today we are going to map your mouth so you have full control."
* *Articulators Intro:* Show him where the tongue tip, blade, and back are.
* *Diagnostic Drill:* Record him reading a basic social sentence: *"We sat in the garden and had casual conversations until late."* Check if he drops the `-t` on *sat*, the `-n` on *garden*, the `-l` on *casual*, the `-z` on *conversations*, or the `-t` on *late*.

#### 3. Drills (15 mins)
* **Articulator Targets:**
  * Alveolar ridge contact: **let** `/let/`, **bet** `/bet/`, **late** `/leɪt/`
  * Soft palate (velum) contact: **pocket** `/ˈpɒkɪt/`, **rabbit** `/ˈræbɪt/`, **pack** `/pæk/`
  * Lip contact: **habit** `/ˈhæbɪt/`, **visit** `/ˈvɪzɪt/`, **field** `/fiːld/`
* **Social Drills:** 
  * *"Let's go for a walk."* `/lets ɡəʊ fər ə wɔːk/`
  * *"It is a habit of mine."* `/ɪt ɪz ə ˈhæbɪt əv maɪn/`
  * *"We will visit the park."* `/wiː wɪl ˈvɪzɪt ðə pɑːk/`

#### 4. Vinh's Cheat Sheet & Homework
* **Physical Cue:** Practice tapping the tongue tip on the alveolar ridge 10 times morning and night.
* **Homework:** Record reading the diagnostic sentence 3 times. Focus on making sure the tongue touches the roof of the mouth at the end of *sat*, *garden*, and *late*.
* **Recommended Video:** Search YouTube for *"Geoff Lindsey - Received Pronunciation"* to understand the modern RP soundscape.

### Lesson 2: Stopping the Drop - Final Plosives & Vowel Clipping
* **Focus:** Final stops `/p, t, k/` (fortis/voiceless) and `/b, d, ɡ/` (lenis/voiced) (Roach Chapters 4 & 5).
* **Linguistic Detail:** **Pre-fortis clipping**. Vowels are shortened before voiceless final consonants (`/p, t, k/`) and lengthened before voiced ones (`/b, d, ɡ/`).
* **Vietnamese Transfer:** Vinh will tend to keep his mouth closed at the end of words (unreleased stops) or drop the sound. He needs to *release* the air.

#### 1. Warm-up Exercises (3 mins)
* **The Tissue Blow:** Hold a tissue 5 cm from the mouth. Say `/p! t! k!/` and make the tissue move with the release of air.
* **The Motorboat:** Brr-brr-brr lips together to activate bilabial plosives (/p, b/).

#### 2. Tutor Plan & Script (20 mins)
* *Concept:* "Vinh, in English, we know a consonant is voiceless because the vowel before it is clipped short. Look at **bet** vs. **bed**. In **bet**, the vowel is short. In **bed**, we stretch the vowel. Let's practice releasing the final consonant."
* *Visual Cue:* Show a quick release of the tongue tip from the alveolar ridge for `/t/` and `/d/`.

#### 3. Drills (15 mins)
* **Vowel Length & Release Pairs:**
  * **bet** `/bet/` (short vowel, air puff) vs. **bed** `/bed/` (long vowel, voiced release)
  * **late** `/leɪt/` (short) vs. **laid** `/leɪd/` (long)
  * **tap** `/tæp/` (short) vs. **tab** `/tæb/` (long)
  * **pack** `/pæk/` (short) vs. **bag** `/bæɡ/` (long)
* **Social Drills:** 
  * *"I laid down on the bed."* `/aɪ leɪd daʊn ɒn ðə bed/`
  * *"Let's make a bet."* `/lets meɪk ə bet/`

#### 4. Vinh's Cheat Sheet & Homework
* **Physical Cue:** Place your hand on your throat. Feel the vibration for `/d, b, ɡ/` and the absence of vibration but stronger air puff for `/t, p, k/`.
* **Homework:** Practice the 4 minimal pairs above. Use a tissue to ensure the voiceless stops are released.
* **Video:** Search YouTube for *"BBC Learning English - Pronunciation Workshops: Plosives"*.

### Lesson 3: The Hiss and the Buzz - Final Fricatives /f, v, s, z/
* **Focus:** Sustaining continuous airflow at the end of words (Roach Chapter 6).
* **Linguistic Detail:** Contrast between voiceless `/s, f/` and voiced `/z, v/`. The vowel before `/s, f/` is clipped; before `/z, v/` it is long.
* **Vietnamese Transfer:** Vietnamese has no final fricatives. Vinh will drop them (e.g., *bus* -> "bu") or replace them with a stop (e.g., *bus* -> "but").

#### 1. Warm-up Exercises (3 mins)
* **The Balloon Leak:** Take a deep breath and hiss like a deflating balloon: `/ssssssssss/` for 10 seconds without stopping.
* **The Snake to Bee:** Alternate from voiceless 'ssss' to voiced 'zzzz' without stopping the airflow.

#### 2. Tutor Plan & Script (20 mins)
* *Concept:* "Fricatives are friction sounds. The air must *never* stop flowing. If you stop the air, it sounds like a plosive. Keep the air leaking out like a tiny balloon tear."
* *Mouth Shape:* For `/f, v/`, touch lower lip to upper teeth. For `/s, z/`, bring teeth close together, tongue tip near the alveolar ridge, blow air through the center groove.

#### 3. Drills (15 mins)
* **Continuous Air Pairs:**
  * **price** `/praɪs/` (short vowel, hissing `/s/`) vs. **prize** `/praɪz/` (long vowel, buzzing `/z/`)
  * **safe** `/seɪf/` (short vowel, blowing `/f/`) vs. **save** `/seɪv/` (long vowel, voiced `/v/`)
  * **fuss** `/fʌs/` vs. **fuzz** `/fʌz/`
  * **face** `/feɪs/` vs. **phase** `/feɪz/`
* **Social Drills:** 
  * *"We will save the prize."* `/wiː wɪl seɪv ðə praɪz/`
  * *"Is it safe to face the crowd?"* `/ɪz ɪt seɪf tə feɪs ðə kraʊd/`

#### 4. Vinh's Cheat Sheet & Homework
* **Physical Cue:** For `/z/`, touch your fingers to your larynx (throat) and make a "bee buzz" sound. Ensure the buzz lasts for at least 2 seconds at the end of *prize* and *phase*.
* **Homework:** Record saying: *"We need to save the prize."* Focus on making the ending sounds buzz.
* **Video:** Search YouTube for *"Rachel's English - How to Pronounce S and Z"*.

### Lesson 4: The Social Softeners - Advanced Fricatives & Affricates
* **Focus:** Dental fricatives `/θ, ð/`, post-alveolar fricatives `/ʃ, ʒ/`, and affricates `/tʃ, dʒ/` (Roach Chapters 6 & 7).
* **Linguistic Detail:** Clean articulation of the dental friction for `/θ/` (*bath, health*) and the complex stop-release of affricates `/tʃ/` (*beach*) and `/dʒ/` (*bridge*).
* **Vietnamese Transfer:** Vinh will likely replace `/θ/` with `/t/` or `/s/` (*bath* -> "bat" or "bas"), and drop final affricates or replace `/dʒ/` with `/z/` (*bridge* -> "briz").

#### 1. Warm-up Exercises (3 mins)
* **The Lizard Tongue:** Stick the tongue tip out between the teeth and blow gently to create a soft, friction-less breeze (`/θθθθθθ/`).
* **The Train Chug:** Say 'tsh-tsh-tsh-tsh' like a steam engine to warm up affricates.

#### 2. Tutor Plan & Script (20 mins)
* *Concept:* "Vinh, everyday conversations are full of words like **bath**, **health**, and **bridge**. These sounds require specific tongue gestures. For **bath**, the tongue tip must peek out between your teeth. For **bridge**, we stop the air behind the teeth, then release it with a wet friction sound."

#### 3. Drills (15 mins)
* **Fricative & Affricate Targets:**
  * **bath** `/bɑːθ/` (Note the RP bath vowel `/ɑː/` + tongue-tip `/θ/`)
  * **health** `/helθ/`
  * **beach** `/biːtʃ/`
  * **bridge** `/brɪdʒ/`
  * **casual** `/ˈkæʒuəl/` (Note the voiced `/ʒ/` in the middle)
  * **conversation** `/ˌkɒnvəˈseɪʃn̩/` (Note the voiceless `/ʃ/` in the suffix)
* **Social Drills:** 
  * *"We had a casual conversation on the bridge."* `/wiː hæd ə ˈkæʒuəl ˌkɒnvəˈseɪʃn̩ ɒn ðə brɪdʒ/`
  * *"A hot bath is good for your health."* `/ə hɒt bɑːθ ɪz ɡʊd fə jɔː helθ/`

#### 4. Vinh's Cheat Sheet & Homework
* **Physical Cue:** Look in a mirror. For *bath*, ensure you can see the tip of your tongue touch your top teeth. For *bridge*, press the tongue flat, then pull it back sharply.
* **Homework:** Practice reading: *"A hot bath is good for your health."*
* **Video:** Search YouTube for *"Geoff Lindsey - Dental Fricatives (TH)"*.

### Lesson 5: Suffix Hurdles - Plurals & Third Person /s, z/ Clusters
* **Focus:** Suffix grammar endings (`-s` / `-es`) and consonant clusters (Roach Chapter 8).
* **Linguistic Detail:** Syllable coda structure. The suffix rule:
  1. Voiceless ending -> `/s/` (*cats* `/kæts/`, *packs* `/pæks/`)
  2. Voiced ending -> `/z/` (*beds* `/bedz/`, *farms* `/fɑːmz/`)
  3. Sibilant ending -> `/ɪz/` (*houses* `/ˈhaʊzɪz/`, *bridges* `/ˈbrɪdʒɪz/`)
* **Vietnamese Transfer:** Vinh has zero consonant clusters in his native language. He will omit the final suffix sound.

#### 1. Warm-up Exercises (3 mins)
* **The Chain Exercise:** Practice adding consonants step-by-step:
  * *gate* -> *gates* ( alveolar `/t/` -> friction `/s/` )
  * *friend* -> *friends* ( alveolar `/d/` -> vocal buzz `/z/` )

#### 2. Tutor Plan & Script (20 mins)
* *Concept:* "Vinh, plurals and verb endings are grammatical. If you drop them, native speakers hear grammatical errors. We are going to build consonant clusters like blocks. First say the base word: **friend**. Hold the `/d/`. Now, add the buzz `/z/`: **friends**."

#### 3. Drills (15 mins)
* **Voicing Categories:**
  * **Category 1 (/s/):** **cats** `/kæts/`, **packs** `/pæks/`, **gates** `/ɡeɪts/`
  * **Category 2 (/z/):** **beds** `/bedz/`, **farms** `/fɑːmz/`, **friends** `/frendz/`
  * **Category 3 (/ɪz/):** **houses** `/ˈhaʊzɪz/`, **bridges** `/ˈbrɪdʒɪz/`
* **Social Drills:** 
  * *"My friends visited the farms."* `/maɪ frendz ˈvɪzɪtɪd ðə fɑːmz/`
  * *"The cats sat near the gates."* `/ðə kæts sæt nɪə ðə ɡeɪts/`

#### 4. Vinh's Cheat Sheet & Homework
* **Physical Cue:** Break down the cluster: *"ca-t-s"*. Connect them slowly: *cat...s*. Slide from the stop `/t/` directly into the hiss `/s/`.
* **Homework:** Practice the "voicing category" list. Record yourself reading: *"My friends visited the farms and saw many cats."*
* **Video:** Search YouTube for *"Rachel's English - How to Pronounce Plural nouns"*.

### Lesson 6: Past-Tense Traps - Final /t, d/ Clusters
* **Focus:** Past tense grammar endings (`-ed`) and coda clusters (Roach Chapter 8).
* **Linguistic Detail:** The three past-tense ending rules:
  1. Voiceless base -> `/t/` (*cooked* `/kʊkt/`, *baked* `/beɪkt/`)
  2. Voiced base -> `/d/` (*bridged* `/brɪdʒd/`, *lived* `/lɪvd/`, *closed* `/kləʊzd/`)
  3. Alveolar stop base (`/t, d/`) -> `/ɪd/` (*visited* `/ˈvɪzɪtɪd/`, *started* `/ˈstɑːtɪd/`)
* **Vietnamese Transfer:** Vinh will skip the `-ed` ending completely to avoid making the consonant cluster.

#### 1. Warm-up Exercises (3 mins)
* **The Step-Down:** Practice transitioning from a fricative to a stop: `/s/` to `/t/` (say *"ssss-t, ssss-t"*), or `/v/` to `/d/` (say *"vvvv-d, vvvv-d"*).

#### 2. Tutor Plan & Script (20 mins)
* *Concept:* "Just like plurals, past tense endings are crucial for sharing stories. Saying 'Yesterday we cook dinner' sounds incomplete. We must pronounce the `/t/` or `/d/` at the end of **cooked** or **closed**."

#### 3. Drills (15 mins)
* **Voicing Categories:**
  * **Category 1 (/t/):** **cooked** `/kʊkt/`, **baked** `/beɪkt/`
  * **Category 2 (/d/):** **bridged** `/brɪdʒd/`, **lived** `/lɪvd/`, **closed** `/kləʊzd/`
  * **Category 3 (/ɪd/):** **visited** `/ˈvɪzɪtɪd/`, **started** `/ˈstɑːtɪd/`
* **Social Drills:** 
  * *"We cooked dinner and closed the door."* `/wiː kʊkt ˈdɪnər ənd kləʊzd ðə dɔː/`
  * *"They started baking when we visited."* `/ðeɪ ˈstɑːtɪd ˈbeɪkɪŋ wen wiː ˈvɪzɪtɪd/`

#### 4. Vinh's Cheat Sheet & Homework
* **Physical Cue:** If a word ends in `/t/` or `/d/` already, you *must* add an extra syllable (`/ɪd/`). For other words, do not add an extra syllable, just tap the final stop.
* **Homework:** Record reading: *"We cooked dinner and closed the door when our friends visited."*
* **Video:** Search YouTube for *"BBC Learning English - Pronunciation of -ed"*

### Lesson 7: The Rhythm of Speech - Weak Syllables & Schwa
* **Focus:** Stress-timed rhythm, vowel reduction to the schwa `/ə/`, and syllabic consonants (Roach Chapters 9, 10, & 11).
* **Linguistic Detail:** Stress contrast. Stressed syllables are loud, long, and clear. Unstressed syllables contain reduced vowels (usually `/ə/` or `/ɪ/`) or syllabic consonants (`/l̩/` or `/n̩/`).
* **Vietnamese Transfer:** Syllable-timed speaking. Vinh will pronounce *vacation* as "VA-CA-TION" with equal stress. By reducing unstressed syllables, he will gain the time needed to pronounce ending consonants in stressed syllables.

#### 1. Warm-up Exercises (3 mins)
* **The Drummer:** Tap your desk on stressed syllables only: **DIN**-ner (TAP - silent), va-**CA**-tion (silent - TAP - silent).
* **The Relaxed Sigh:** Relax the jaw, keep the tongue flat, and make a lazy, effort-free grunt: `/ə, ə, ə/`.

#### 2. Tutor Plan & Script (20 mins)
* *Concept:* "Vinh, English is a rhythm game. Stressed syllables are like the major beats in music; unstressed syllables are squished. We reduce vowels to a lazy sound: the **schwa** `/ə/`. It lets you speak faster and focus on ending sounds."

#### 3. Drills (15 mins)
* **Schwa & Syllabic Consonant Words:**
  * **habit** `/ˈhæbɪt/`
  * **dinner** `/ˈdɪnə/` (Reduce second syllable to schwa; no R in RP)
  * **garden** `/ˈɡɑːdn̩/` (Syllabic `/n̩/` at the end; no vowel)
  * **cushion** `/ˈkʊʃn̩/`
  * **vacation** `/vəˈkeɪʃn̩/` (Schwa in first syllable; syllabic `/n̩/` in third)
* **Social Drills:** 
  * *"We had dinner in the garden."* `/wiː hæd ˈdɪnər ɪn ðə ˈɡɑːdn̩/`
  * *"We went on a summer vacation."* `/wiː went ɒn ə ˈsʌmə vəˈkeɪʃn̩/`

#### 4. Vinh's Cheat Sheet & Homework
* **Physical Cue:** Keep your mouth almost closed and tongue relaxed to make the schwa `/ə/`. It requires zero muscular effort.
* **Homework:** Practice tapping the rhythm of: *"We had **DIN**-ner in the **GAR**-den during **SUM**-mer."*
* **Video:** Search YouTube for *"Geoff Lindsey - The Schwa"* or *"Rachel's English - Syllabic Consonants"*.

### Lesson 8: Connected Flow - Linking & Elision
* **Focus:** Word-to-word transitions, linking consonants to vowels, linking `/r/`, intrusive `/r/`, and alveolar elision (Roach Chapters 12 & 14).
* **Linguistic Detail:**
  1. Consonant-to-Vowel linking: *lot of* -> "lo-tov"
  2. Linking `/r/`: In RP, final "r" is silent unless followed by a vowel (*clear* `/klɪə/` vs. *clear answer* `/klɪər ˈɑːnsə/`)
  3. Elision of `/t, d/`: Omitting `/t/` or `/d/` in a cluster when followed by a consonant (*last night* -> "las-night"). This is a native-speaker shortcut Vinh can use!
* **Vietnamese Transfer:** Vinh tends to pronounce words in isolation, inserting a tiny pause (glottal stop) between words. Linking will make his English flow naturally and make ending consonants easier to pronounce.

#### 1. Warm-up Exercises (3 mins)
* **The Slide:** Say "My name is... Vinh" without any gap, sliding the `/z/` of *is* directly into the *V* of *Vinh*.
* **The Sound Jump:** Link final 's' to vowels, e.g., 'thisIs' -> 'thi-sis'.

#### 2. Tutor Plan & Script (20 mins)
* *Concept:* "Vinh, native speakers don't talk word-by-word. We sing our sentences. The ending consonant of one word hops onto the front of the next word if it starts with a vowel. Look at **lot of**. We say **lo-tov**."

#### 3. Drills (15 mins)
* **Linking Patterns:**
  * **lot of** -> *"lo-tov"* (`/lɒt ɒv/`)
  * **late arrival** -> *"late-tuh-rival"* (`/leɪt əˈraɪvl̩/`)
  * **sofa cushion** -> *"sofa-r-cushion"* (`/ˈsəʊfə ˈkʊʃn̩/` - RP intrusive `/r/`)
  * **clear answer** -> *"clear-rahn-suh"* (`/klɪər ˈɑːnsə/` - RP linking `/r/`)
  * **last night** -> *"las-night"* (elision of `/t/` before consonant `/n/`)
  * **old friend** -> *"owl-friend"* (elision of `/d/` before consonant `/f/`)
* **Social Drills:** 
  * *"We saw a lot of animals last night."* `/wiː sɔː ə lɒt ɒv ˈænɪmlz lɑːs naɪt/`
  * *"An old friend gave a clear answer."* `/ən əʊl frend ɡeɪv ə klɪər ˈɑːnsə/`

#### 4. Vinh's Cheat Sheet & Homework
* **Physical Cue:** Imagine there are no spaces between words in a sentence. Read sentences as if they are one long word.
* **Homework:** Record reading: *"An old friend gave a clear answer last night."*
* **Video:** Search YouTube for *"BBC Learning English - Connected Speech Linking"*.

### Lesson 9: The Weekend Picnic - Capstone Integration
* **Focus:** Capstone integration, RP accent features, and casual storytelling delivery (Roach Chapters 15-20).
* **Linguistic Detail:** Synthesizing final consonant releases, vowel clipping, suffix clusters, schwa reduction, and linking in a relaxed, friendly social storytelling context.
* **RP Specific Review:**
  * Non-rhoticity: Ensure post-vocalic 'r' is silent (*farms* `/fɑːmz/`, *dinner* `/ˈdɪnə/`).
  * Bath Vowel: Use `/ɑː/` in *garden, bath, farm*.
  * Goat Vowel: Keep `/əʊ/` clean in *sofa, boat, local*.

#### 1. Warm-up Exercises (3 mins)
* **The RP Vowel Cascade:** Say `/ɑː/` (long open ah), `/əʊ/` (goat), `/ɪə/` (near) to tune the mouth for British RP resonances.
* **The Tall Breath:** Breathe in deeply, expanding the ribs to create warm, open vocal resonance.

#### 2. Tutor Plan & Script (20 mins)
* *Concept:* "Vinh, today is your capstone lesson. We are going to practice a friendly storytelling passage using your beautiful, clean RP accent. Focus on every final consonant, your stress rhythm, and linking words together smoothly."

#### 3. Drills (15 mins)
* **Capstone Key Words:**
  * **vacation** `/vəˈkeɪʃn̩/`
  * **holiday** `/ˈhɒlɪdeɪ/`
  * **casual** `/ˈkæʒuəl/`
  * **relaxing** `/rɪˈlæksɪŋ/`
  * **conversations** `/ˌkɒnvəˈseɪʃnz/`
* **Capstone Narrative Drill:**
  * Read the following passage with perfect phonetic precision:
  * *"My **friends** `/frendz/` and I planned a **relaxing** `/rɪˈlæksɪŋ/` **vacation** `/vəˈkeɪʃn̩/` in the countryside. We **cooked** `/kʊkt/` a **lot of** `/lɒt ɒv/` food and **visited** `/ˈvɪzɪtɪd/` the local **farms** `/fɑːmz/` **last night** `/lɑːs naɪt/`. We sat in the **garden** `/ˈɡɑːdn̩/` and had **casual** `/ˈkæʒuəl/` **conversations** `/ˌkɒnvəˈseɪʃnz/` until **late** `/leɪt/`."*

#### 4. Vinh's Cheat Sheet & Homework
* **Physical Cue:** Take a deep breath, lift the soft palate, anchor the voice, and release each ending sound calmly.
* **Homework:** Record the capstone narrative passage. Compare it to your Lesson 1 diagnostic recording to hear the contrast.
* **Video:** Search YouTube for *"Geoff Lindsey - Modern British Pronunciation"*.
