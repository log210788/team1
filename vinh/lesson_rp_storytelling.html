RP Accent Foundations

Teach these core rules before reading. Click any rule card to hear the sound demonstration.

1. The Broad 'A' Sound
/ɑː/

Words like *ask, path, bath, glass, last* are pronounced with a long, back-of-the-throat "ah".

2. Non-Rhoticity (Silent 'R')
/r/ omission

Do NOT pronounce the "R" unless followed by a vowel. e.g. *north* -> /nɔːθ/, *warm* -> /wɔːm/.

3. Crisp Consonant /t/
Crisp Plosives

Ensure /t/ is pronounced clearly. Do not slide into a flap [d] (e.g. *disputing* not *dispuding*).

4. Storytelling Pacing
Intonation

Use a wide pitch range. Pitch peaks for new information, drops for side notes. Pause at punctuation.

1. Linking Sounds

Connect ending consonants to beginning vowels (e.g. *tell~a*). Adds glide sounds: *he[j]is*, *go[w]out*, *law[r]and*.

"build~a~story"

2. Blending (Coalescence)

When sounds merge, such as /d/+/j/ -> /dʒ/ or /t/+/j/ -> /tʃ/. Seen inside words (soldier, educate, pressure) and across words (would you).

"soldier" • "treasure" • "educate"

3. Weak Forms

Grammar words (*to, for, can, and, of*) lose stress. The vowel reduces to the neutral schwa sound.

"can" (/kən/) • "to" (/tə/)

4. Elision (Dropping)

The omission of a sound (often /t/ or /d/) when trapped between other consonants. Makes clusters easier.

"nex(t)~class"
